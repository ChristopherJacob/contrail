Contrail

Flights overhead, on a touch screen that costs about $15.

Open-source firmware for the ESP32 “Cheap Yellow Display”. It shows the planes above you, live from free ADS-B feeds or your own receiver, with smooth 30 fps animation and a sky that follows the sun.

Install from your browser Source on GitHub

Seven screens, two themes

Each screen in Cinematic Sky (left) and Bold Editorial (right), straight from the firmware's renderer. Contrail picks the screen from what is happening, cycles them, or stays put; a tap moves between them.

Features

  • Free live dataCommunity ADS-B feeds (adsb.lol, adsb.fi, airplanes.live) with no key, your own dump1090 / readsb receiver, or OpenSky. Routes from adsbdb.
  • Smooth graphics30 fps animation on a board with no PSRAM, drawn in strips with only the changed pixels sent to the screen.
  • A sky that follows the sunDay, dusk and night skies from the sun's real position, with the moon, its phase and the stars.
  • Two themesCinematic Sky, and Bold Editorial in flat airline colours and big type.
  • AlertsEmergency squawks, military aircraft, a watchlist of tails, callsigns and types, and low passes, each switched on or off.
  • Set up from your phoneWi-Fi setup on first boot, then a web page on the board for settings, the watchlist and firmware updates. Quick settings on the touch screen.

Hardware

Contrail runs on the ESP32-2432S028R, the 2.8-inch “Cheap Yellow Display”: an ESP32 with a 240×320 touch screen, an RGB LED and a microSD slot, all on one board. It is sold by many shops for about $15; search for ESP32-2432S028R. Both the version with two USB ports (USB-C and micro-USB) and the original micro-USB one work.

A microSD card is optional. With one, Contrail shows real airline logos instead of drawn tail fins, and a detailed Live Map with roads, rivers, towns and every airfield around you. Build the card in your browser.
